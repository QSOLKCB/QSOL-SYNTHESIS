# Abstract

QSOL-SYNTHESIS asks how the major QSOL-IMC projects relate to one another and which ideas recur across their repositories and publications. The current public corpus spans information dynamics, astrophysical modelling, quantum and qudit simulation, error-correction evidence, sonification, machine perception, language-model experiments, persistent multi-agent systems, software preservation, and portability engineering.

The first-pass synthesis finds a recurring methodological concern that can be described as **trustworthy transformation**. Across otherwise different domains, projects repeatedly distinguish a source or state from the transformation applied to it, the observation produced from it, the validation used to compare results, and the interpretation eventually attached to those results. Closely related motifs include representation versus referent, deterministic replay versus truth, oracle/candidate parity, provenance, recovery, externalised state, authority partitioning, falsification, and explicit nonclaims.

This is a structural synthesis, not a proposed common physical theory. A repeated workflow shape does not establish shared mechanism or ontology. Quantum error correction, historical software compatibility, semantic relay experiments, and sonification can exhibit comparable validation architectures while remaining physically and scientifically distinct.

The strongest working proposition is therefore limited: QSOL-IMC repeatedly develops ways to preserve meaningful structure under transformation while making the transformation, evidence boundary, and claim ceiling inspectable. The repository tests that proposition against first-party public sources, records counterexamples and gaps, and keeps raw discovery separate from curated synthesis.
